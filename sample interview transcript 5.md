# User Interview Transcript 5

---

**Interviewer:** Thanks for talking with me. Can you tell me about the last group project you worked on?

**Student:** Yeah. It was a marketing strategy project, and honestly it started the way all of them start, with everybody sounding very responsible for about twenty minutes.

**Interviewer:** What happens after those twenty minutes?

**Student:** People get busy. Which sounds obvious, but that's kind of the whole thing. Everyone has other classes, jobs, clubs. So the project doesn't fall apart because nobody cares. It falls apart because coordination is fragile.

**Interviewer:** How did that show up in this project?

**Student:** We split work into sections, but the scope of the sections wasn't actually that clear. Like someone says "I'll do customer research," but that could mean very different things. A paragraph? A whole slide section? Gathering sources? People think they agreed when they didn't.

**Interviewer:** So maybe task definition is the main issue.

**Student:** It's one issue. But again, if the group had a clear shared place showing what each task actually meant, it would be less of a problem.

**Interviewer:** Did you have any shared place?

**Student:** Technically yes. Reality, no. We had a doc and a chat. The doc was where things were supposed to become organized. The chat was where life actually happened.

**Interviewer:** What do you mean by "life actually happened"?

**Student:** Last-minute changes, people saying they're behind, someone asking if the professor changed the requirements, rescheduling, random side comments. All the stuff that changes the project state happens in conversation.

**Interviewer:** Was there a moment where that caused trouble?

**Student:** Yeah. I remember one person saying they were "basically done" with their section. Then later we found out they meant they had notes, not polished slides. Which, to be fair, maybe that was obvious to them. It wasn't obvious to the rest of us.

**Interviewer:** "Basically done" sounds dangerous.

**Student:** Exactly. Group projects are full of fake certainty words.

**Interviewer:** That's good. What did you do when you realized the section wasn't actually done?

**Student:** I think I messaged them directly instead of in the group. Which happens a lot. Once the shared system feels unreliable, people start doing side-channel management.

**Interviewer:** Why direct message instead of the group?

**Student:** Because it feels faster. Also less embarrassing maybe. But then that makes the shared understanding problem worse, because now important coordination is happening privately.

**Interviewer:** So privacy fragments the workflow.

**Student:** Yes, but I wouldn't have said it like that. I would just say everyone starts patching the system in their own way.

**Interviewer:** Fair. Did anyone try a tool like Trello or Notion?

**Student:** A couple times in different classes. Same pattern every time. People set it up when they're optimistic, then stop maintaining it when the semester gets busy.

**Interviewer:** Is that because the tools are too heavy?

**Student:** Heavy, or just disconnected from where attention already is. Students live in messaging. Asking them to maintain a separate source of truth is hard unless it saves obvious effort.

**Interviewer:** So if something reduced effort inside the existing workflow, that would be more attractive?

**Student:** Yes. Especially if it could turn vague conversation into something clearer. Like, okay, here is what we actually agreed on. Here are the unresolved things. Here is who still owes what.

**Interviewer:** That sounds almost like an account manager for the group.

**Student:** Maybe, as long as it doesn't feel bossy. I don't want software scolding college students in a group chat.

**Interviewer:** That's fair.

**Student:** Also, one more thing. Sometimes people don't even know they're blocked. They think they're waiting responsibly, but really nobody else knows they're waiting.

**Interviewer:** That's important. So blockage visibility matters too.

**Student:** Yes, definitely. A lot of the stress is invisible dependencies.

**Interviewer:** Anything else before we wrap?

**Student:** Just that students don't need a better planning ceremony. They need a better sense of what is true right now.
