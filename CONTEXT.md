# Context

Use this worksheet to capture the minimum context needed before planning or running user interviews.

## Research
**Research Question**
> Is managing group projects painful enough for college students that they actively want a better solution?

**Decision to Inform**
> Whether this problem is strong enough to justify exploring a new solution.

**What We Already Know**
> Students often coordinate group work through chat, which makes task ownership and accountability hard to track consistently.

**Assumption to Test**
> We believe group project coordination is painful enough that college students actively want a better solution.

**What Would Prove Us Wrong**
> Students say group project coordination is annoying but not important enough to change their behavior or adopt a new tool.

## Company
**Team Goal**
> Explore whether this is a meaningful startup opportunity worth pursuing.

**Why This Matters Now**
> The team sees repeated coordination pain in student group work and wants to understand whether it is strong enough to justify building a product.

**Current Constraints**
> The team is still early, so they need to focus on a narrow problem and avoid building something students do not care enough about.

**Potential Differentiation**
> It aims to be lightweight and easy to adopt, rather than feeling like a full project management tool.

## Product
**Product Concept**
> A web-based tool to help college students manage group projects.

**Core User Outcome**
> It helps students keep track of who owns what, what is done, and what still needs attention in a group project.

**Current Pain Point**
> Students lack a lightweight, consistently used way to track task ownership and accountability.

**Current Workarounds**
> Group chat, shared docs, ad hoc reminders, and relying on one organized teammate.

**Initial Solution Direction**
> A simple shared task board for group work.

## Participant
**Target Participant**
> College students who regularly do group projects.

**Current Goal**
> Finish the project successfully, divide work clearly, and avoid last-minute confusion.

**Current Behavior**
> They coordinate through chat, shared docs, and scattered follow-ups.

**Main Frustration**
> Tasks and ownership get lost in conversation, so it is hard to tell what is done and who is accountable.

**Good Fit for This Study**
> They are a college student, have recent group project experience, and use chat to coordinate with teammates.

**Who to Exclude**
> Students who rarely do group projects or already use a formal project management tool consistently.
