# User Interview Transcript 4

---

**Interviewer:** Thanks for doing this. Can you tell me about the last group project you worked on?

**Student:** Yeah, it was a campaign presentation for a communications class. Honestly, with those projects I usually end up caring less about the creative part and more about whether the group is actually functioning.

**Interviewer:** What do you mean by "functioning"?

**Student:** Like, does everyone know the plan, do they know when something changed, does anybody actually know who is doing the intro versus just assuming that got decided somewhere.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through how the project started.

**Student:** We had a quick planning meeting after class. It felt productive. Someone took notes, I think in a Google Doc. We split up research, slide design, speaking parts. Pretty standard.

**Interviewer:** And then?

**Student:** Then reality happened. People started messaging instead of updating the doc. Also, one person missed the first meeting, so from the beginning they were sort of catching up through screenshots and random summaries from people.

**Interviewer:** Did that create confusion?

**Student:** Yeah, but not all at once. More like a low-level fog the whole time. Sorry, that's dramatic, but that's what it feels like.

**Interviewer:** No, that's good. Can you give me an example of the fog?

**Student:** Sure. At one point I thought we had decided on one campaign angle, and then in a later message thread it became clear two people were still working from an older version of the idea. Nobody formally said "this is now the direction." It just sort of shifted.

**Interviewer:** So was the problem decision-making?

**Student:** Decision-making, but really decision visibility. We did make decisions. They just didn't stay visible.

**Interviewer:** That's helpful.

**Student:** Also, sorry, another thing is that there is almost always one person everyone starts relying on.

**Interviewer:** Like a leader?

**Student:** Not always officially. More like the person who seems organized enough that everyone assumes they know what's going on. Then that person becomes the fallback system.

**Interviewer:** Were you that person?

**Student:** Unfortunately, yes, for that project.

**Interviewer:** What did that look like?

**Student:** People would DM me instead of asking in the group chat. Like, "Wait, are we meeting Thursday?" "Did we decide who is presenting results?" "Is the professor okay with this angle?" After a while I was basically translating the state of the group for everyone.

**Interviewer:** Did that make you feel in control, or burdened?

**Student:** Both? At first it feels good because you're the one who knows what's happening. Then it gets annoying because now the system depends on one person's memory.

**Interviewer:** That's interesting. So maybe the real issue is leadership load.

**Student:** I mean, partly. But if the information were clearer, the leadership load would matter less. The reason people leaned on me is that the group itself wasn't legible.

**Interviewer:** Legible is a good word.

**Student:** Yeah. Like, a readable group.

**Interviewer:** Did anyone try tools beyond chat and docs?

**Student:** Someone made a spreadsheet once. I actually liked it. But then people stopped checking it because all the active conversation was still in iMessage.

**Interviewer:** So the chat always wins.

**Student:** Pretty much. Unless the other tool is somehow tied into the chat or easier than the chat, which is hard.

**Interviewer:** If a tool lived inside the chat, would that solve it?

**Student:** Maybe some of it. I think the main thing would be surfacing decisions and ownership automatically. Not making us "manage a project" in some formal way.

**Interviewer:** What would you want surfaced?

**Student:** Who owns what, what changed, and what is still undecided. Also maybe when people are working off outdated assumptions, if that's even possible.

**Interviewer:** That's useful. Would reminders help?

**Student:** Maybe, but reminders are not the first thing I would fix. The first thing is shared understanding.

**Interviewer:** Got it. Anything else that stands out?

**Student:** Just that group projects feel bad when you're never sure whether the calm is real or fake. Sometimes everyone is quiet because things are fine. Sometimes everyone is quiet because everyone assumes someone else is handling it.
