# Bullseye Customer Question Bank

This reference is based on Michael Margolis's "Questions to Define your Bullseye Customer" worksheet from LearnMoreFaster.com and the shared Google Sheet version of that worksheet. Use it to move from a loose ICP idea to measurable recruiting criteria.

## How to Use

1. Run a 45-minute working session with the core team.
2. Answer only the categories relevant to the product and market.
3. Push every answer toward measurable, observable criteria.
4. Capture three outputs:
   - inclusion criteria
   - exclusion criteria
   - trigger events
5. If two distinct bullseye groups emerge, split them.

## Question Categories

### 1. Basic profile

Use this category to describe the core attributes, needs, behaviors, and attitudes of the target customer.

Prompts:
- What core traits define this customer?
- What recurring needs or pains do they have?
- What habits make them more likely to adopt?
- What attitudes or preferences matter?

### 2. New vs. existing users

Use this category to decide whether the best participants are already familiar with the company or product.

Prompts:
- Are these people existing customers or completely new prospects?
- If existing, how long have they used the product?
- If new, what prior knowledge should they have?

### 3. Sector or industry

Use for B2B products that fit some industries better than others.

Prompts:
- Which industries are most likely to care?
- Which sectors should be excluded for now?
- Are there regulatory or workflow reasons to focus narrowly?

### 4. Use of related or competitive products

Use this category to identify tools, substitutes, and habits that shape expectations.

Prompts:
- What products should they already use?
- What products should they know about?
- Which tools would make them a poor fit?

### 5. Disqualifying personal experiences

Use this category to identify life experiences that could bias reactions or make the person atypical.

Prompts:
- Which personal experiences would make someone unusually negative or unrepresentative?
- Are there situations that would distort how they evaluate the concept?

### 6. Disqualifying professional experience

Use this category to avoid participants who know too much, work too close to the problem, or are constrained by policy.

Prompts:
- Which companies or industries should they not have worked in?
- Are there policy or incentive constraints that matter?
- Would prior insider knowledge bias feedback?

### 7. Titles, roles, and responsibilities

Use this category to define the right level of expertise and day-to-day ownership.

Prompts:
- Which titles, functions, or responsibilities matter?
- Are you targeting managers, execs, or individual contributors?
- What level of experience is required?
- In consumer contexts, who makes the household decision?

### 8. Geography

Use this category when location changes customer behavior or constraints.

Prompts:
- Which regions or markets matter most?
- Which places should be deprioritized for now?
- Does density, climate, or local regulation affect fit?

### 9. Buyers vs. end users

Use this category when the purchaser and day-to-day user are not the same person.

Prompts:
- Is the main learning goal about purchase or usage?
- Do you need the buyer, the user, or both?

### 10. Scale of organization

Use this category in B2B to define the best-fit company size and operational complexity.

Prompts:
- What company size fits best?
- How many employees, customers, or locations matter?
- Does funding stage, revenue, or data volume matter?

### 11. Team structure and distribution

Use this category when collaboration patterns affect product value.

Prompts:
- Are teams centralized or distributed?
- Are they remote, hybrid, or office-based?
- Does reporting structure affect adoption?

### 12. Income and budgets

Use this category to infer purchasing power or budget reality without relying on awkward direct income questions.

Prompts:
- What signs show spending power or budget ownership?
- How price-sensitive are they?
- What purchasing habits indicate fit?

### 13. Life and work setting

Use this category to capture contextual conditions around home, workplace, or operating environment.

Prompts:
- What kind of setting do they live or work in?
- What household or work environment factors matter?
- Are there context clues that signal poor fit?

### 14. Trigger events

Use this category to identify why this customer is likely to care right now.

Prompts:
- What recent change makes them more receptive?
- What event increases urgency?
- What event would make them less likely to care?

### 15. VIP criteria

Use this category when some customers are strategically more valuable than others.

Prompts:
- Which customers matter more to the business right now?
- Does account size, reputation, usage, or influence matter?

### 16. Missing representation

Use this category to make the research sample more inclusive and representative.

Prompts:
- Which demographics are present in the target market?
- Which groups are easy to miss if not specified?
- What inclusion targets should recruiters deliver on?

### 17. Other exclusions

Use this category at the end to catch anything that would make you say, "this person is not actually our bullseye."

Prompts:
- What would disqualify someone in the first five minutes?
- What adjacent segment keeps getting confused with the real target?
- What exceptions should recruiting vendors know up front?

## Measurability Rules

Prefer:
- frequency over adjectives
- recent behavior over self-description
- named tools, roles, and events over abstractions
- criteria a recruiter can verify

Avoid:
- "tech-savvy"
- "forward-thinking"
- "busy"
- "high intent"
- "good fit"

Replace vague labels with concrete thresholds such as:
- used a workflow in the last 30 days
- manages a team of 5 or more
- spends more than 10 hours a week on the problem
- changed vendors in the last 12 months

## Output Format

Turn the session into:
- 5-10 measurable inclusion criteria
- 3-7 exclusion criteria
- 1-3 trigger events
- a short segment label
- a list of screener questions
