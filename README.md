# User Studies Skills

> Please submit an issue if you notice anything that can be improved with these two skills.

This folder is a lightweight starter kit for planning and synthesizing UX or product discovery interviews.

## What is here

- `CONTEXT_TEMPLATE.md`: a blank worksheet for defining the minimum context before interviews
- `CONTEXT.md`: a filled example
- `sample interview transcript 1.md` to `sample interview transcript 6.md`: example transcripts for testing synthesis workflows
- `.cursor/skills/bullseye-customer-definition/SKILL.md`: helps narrow the target participant or ICP
- `.cursor/skills/interview-synthesizer/SKILL.md`: helps synthesize interview transcripts into first-pass findings

## Recommended workflow

### 1. Start with context

Before writing an interview guide or running interviews, fill out `CONTEXT_TEMPLATE.md`.

The goal is not to create a full strategy document. The goal is to capture the minimum useful context:

- the research question
- the decision the research should inform
- what you already know
- the assumption you want to test
- who you want to learn from
- the current pain point and current workaround

Use `CONTEXT.md` as a reference for the level of detail.

### 2. Use the context to shape the study

Once the context is filled out, use it to:

- define the interview objective
- decide who to recruit
- choose what should and should not be asked
- keep the conversation focused on the learning goal

The most important fields are usually:

- `Research Question`
- `Decision to Inform`
- `Assumption to Test`
- `Target Participant`
- `Main Frustration`

### 3. Run and capture interviews

After interviews are completed, save each transcript as a separate file.

Good practice:

- one file per interview
- include speaker labels such as `Interviewer:` and `Participant:`
- keep the wording as close to the original conversation as possible

### 4. Synthesize interviews

Use the interview synthesis workflow after you have multiple transcripts.

The intended use is:

- first-pass clustering of evidence
- surfacing recurring findings
- identifying supporting quotes
- producing a rough readout that a researcher or PM can refine

Treat synthesis output as a starting point, not the final truth.

## How to use the context template well

### Keep it short

A good template is brief and concrete. If a section turns into a long memo, it is probably too broad.

### Focus on decisions

The strongest context files explain what decision the team is trying to make. This keeps interviews from drifting into vague exploration.

### Separate what is known from what is assumed

Do not mix evidence with belief. If something is still a hypothesis, write it as a hypothesis.

### Write participant context in behavior terms

Prefer:

- what they do now
- what frustrates them
- what workaround they already use

Over:

- broad personality descriptions
- demographic detail that does not affect the study

## How the two skills fit in

### 1. Bullseye customer definition

Use `.cursor/skills/bullseye-customer-definition/SKILL.md` when you still need to narrow who the study is for.

This skill helps you define:

- who to recruit first
- who to exclude
- what observable criteria make someone a good fit
- how narrow the initial segment should be

This works especially well after filling out `CONTEXT_TEMPLATE.md`, because the template already captures the problem, product idea, and target participant at a high level.

### 2. Interview synthesizer

Use `.cursor/skills/interview-synthesizer/SKILL.md` after interviews are complete.

This skill helps you produce a first-pass synthesis with:

- recurring findings
- evidence-backed quotes
- implications for the product or startup
- next questions to validate

It is most useful when you have multiple interviews and a clear research question already defined.

## Notes

- The context files are designed for classroom use and lightweight startup discovery.
