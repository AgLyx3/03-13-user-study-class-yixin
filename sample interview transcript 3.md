# User Interview Transcript 3

---

**Interviewer:** Thanks for talking with me. Can you tell me about the last group project you worked on?

**Student:** Yeah, it was for a research methods class. We had to design a small study, collect responses, and then present the results. It wasn't even the hardest assignment content-wise. It was more that everyone had a different idea of how structured the group should be.

**Interviewer:** What do you mean by that?

**Student:** Like, one person wanted deadlines for every tiny thing. Another person was very much like, we'll figure it out, it's fine. And I was somewhere in the middle. So from the start there was this weird mismatch where we all thought we had agreed, but I don't think we meant the same thing.

**Interviewer:** Did you have a plan written down somewhere?

**Student:** Kind of. We had a Google Doc with notes from the first meeting. But then after that most of the actual coordination happened in the chat.

**Interviewer:** What kind of things were happening in the chat?

**Student:** Everything. Like actual task decisions, but also "can someone move our meeting," "I missed class, what did the professor say," random side comments. Which sounds normal, and it is, but then it becomes hard to tell what's a real decision versus just discussion.

**Interviewer:** So was the problem that there was too much chatter?

**Student:** Not exactly. I mean, yes, there was a lot of chatter, but I think the bigger problem was uncertainty. I couldn't tell whether people were actually progressing or whether I just hadn't seen the update.

**Interviewer:** Can you give me a specific moment?

**Student:** Um. Yeah. There was one point where I thought another person was handling participant recruitment because they had said something like "I'll take care of it." But what they meant was they would look into it, not that it was fully owned. We didn't realize that difference until a couple days later.

**Interviewer:** That's interesting. So maybe the issue was vague wording?

**Student:** Partly. But also, nobody went back and made the ownership explicit anywhere. If it had been captured clearly somewhere, maybe the wording wouldn't have mattered as much.

**Interviewer:** What happened once you realized recruitment wasn't really covered?

**Student:** We scrambled. I remember I got kind of annoyed, and then I felt bad because it wasn't like the other person was trying to drop the ball. It was more like we had all been guessing what the group state was.

**Interviewer:** Did someone step in to coordinate?

**Student:** Usually that ends up being me or one other person. Not because we want to be "the leader," exactly. More because if nobody does it, my anxiety goes up.

**Interviewer:** So anxiety is a driver here.

**Student:** Yeah, definitely. Sorry if that's not very product-y.

**Interviewer:** No, that's useful. What do you do when your anxiety goes up in a group project?

**Student:** I start checking on everyone, which I know can be annoying. Like I'll say, "Hey, just confirming, are you still doing X?" And sometimes people respond like I'm overreacting, but then sometimes it turns out they were confused too.

**Interviewer:** Did your group ever try using anything other than chat and docs?

**Student:** I suggested a spreadsheet once. Not even a fancy tool, literally just a table with names, tasks, and status. People said yes, but then no one kept it updated.

**Interviewer:** Was that because students don't like tools?

**Student:** I don't think it's that simple. I think they like tools if the tool clearly reduces work. But if it feels like one more obligation, it dies.

**Interviewer:** That makes sense. When you say you wanted more certainty, what exactly did you want to know?

**Student:** Who owns what. What's actually done. What's still ambiguous. And maybe what changed since the last time I looked, because that's the part that gets exhausting.

**Interviewer:** So change tracking?

**Student:** I guess, yes. But I probably wouldn't have said that. I would just say I don't want to keep reconstructing the project from memory and old messages.

**Interviewer:** That's a strong way to put it.

**Student:** It's true, though. Half the stress is doing detective work.

**Interviewer:** If something summarized the current state for you, would that help?

**Student:** Yes, if it was accurate and simple. Not a huge dashboard. More like, here are the decisions, here is what each person owns, here is what's still unresolved.

**Interviewer:** Would you want it to remind people too?

**Student:** Maybe eventually. But honestly clarity first. Reminders are less useful if the underlying picture is wrong.

**Interviewer:** That's helpful. Anything else before we stop?

**Student:** Just that people always talk about group projects like the problem is laziness. Sometimes it is. But a lot of the time it's ambiguity.
